What it trades, and why

Kraken perpetual futures (all books). Perps are Kraken's margin product: they go long or short with leverage, for 0.05% taker fee. A sell that opens a position is a short: it profits if the coin falls. No coins are held, only cash collateral plus perp positions.

Spot margin: not used. Spot costs 0.80% taker plus 0.01-0.04% rollover every 4 h on margin, about 16x the perp fee for the same long/short exposure. Every backtest showed spot/margin losing to perps after costs.

Stocks: via Kraken stock perps (long and short). The stocks book trades SPY, QQQ, GLD, AAPL, NVDA, GOOGL, AMZN, META, TSLA, AMD, INTC and MU as perps (e.g. PF_SPYXUSD), which track the xStocks 1:1 but can also be shorted, for the same 0.05% fee. It goes long names above their trend while SPY is up, and short names below their trend while SPY is down (half size). Spot xStocks are long-only and cost 0.13% per side, so they are not used.

No fixed splits. Every Monday capital is re-split across books: equal risk per book (lower-vol books get more), tilted toward books whose objective is positive after 3 weeks, floor 5%, cap 45%. A book with no signal gets the floor.

Budget €1,000, converted to USD at the start (Kraken perps settle in USD). Sizes are rounded down to the smallest quantity Kraken accepts, so small targets can round to zero. Total notional is kept under 2x equity (assumed EU retail leverage cap). Paper only: fills at the Kraken Futures mark price plus slippage (5 bp majors, 15 bp others) and the 0.05% taker fee; daily funding is charged from Kraken's own rates. No order ever reaches Kraken.
